*A walkable village center two miles from UNC, where you trade yard space for the ability to leave the car parked.*

Southern Village is a roughly 300 acre planned community in Chapel Hill, inside Orange County and inside town limits, built around a walkable commercial center on Market Street about two miles from the UNC campus. It holds condos, townhomes, and single family homes, an elementary school, and a park and ride lot with fare free transit to campus.

## The number that actually separates Southern Village

Chapel Hill has three planned communities that get compared to each other constantly. Here is the part nobody puts side by side.

| | Southern Village | Meadowmont | Briar Chapel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Months of supply** | About 3 | About 4 | About 4 |
| **Market condition** | Seller's market | Balanced | Balanced |
| **Median sale price** | About $730,000 | About $1,125,000 | $639,680 |
| **County** | Orange | Orange | Chatham |
| **Inside town limits** | Yes | Yes | No |

Southern Village is the tightest of the three. It is also the only one currently sitting in seller's market territory, while the wider Chapel Hill area runs eight to fourteen months of supply in places.

And look at the price column next to the county column. Southern Village is the cheapest way into Chapel Hill town limits and Chapel Hill Carrboro City Schools. Its median sits about $395,000 below Meadowmont's and only about $90,000 above Briar Chapel's, which is in a different county with a different school district entirely.

That combination, town limits and the town school district at a price much closer to the Chatham County side, is the single strongest argument for this neighborhood and almost no one makes it out loud.

## What Southern Village actually is

Southern Village was built as a deliberate argument against the standard 1990s subdivision.

Instead of cul-de-sacs and three car garages facing the street, the developers built front porches, sidewalks on both sides, garages tucked onto rear alleys, and a real commercial town center you can walk to. The planning term for this is neo-traditional or New Urbanist. In practice it means the neighborhood was designed so you could live most of a normal week without getting in the car.

Roughly 300 acres, about 1,150 homes, and around 90 acres left as green space and trails. It sits just south of downtown Chapel Hill off 15-501, about two miles from the UNC campus.

Thirty years in, the trees have filled out and the village center has stayed occupied, which is the part most planned communities never get right.

## Market Street and what daily life looks like

The center of the neighborhood is Market Street, and it is the reason most buyers choose Southern Village over a comparable house somewhere else.

You get a movie theater, restaurants, coffee, a handful of small businesses and services, a church, and a village green that hosts events through the year. Not a strip center you drive to. A walkable block you live next to.

The practical version: you can walk to dinner, walk to a movie, walk your dog on the trails, and walk to the elementary school inside the neighborhood. For a lot of buyers coming from cities where that was normal, this is the only Chapel Hill neighborhood that replicates it.

One real advantage over Meadowmont, which is the usual comparison: Southern Village is compact. At roughly 300 acres with everything concentrated around Market Street, nearly every home in the neighborhood is a genuine walk to the center. Meadowmont covers 435 acres, so its walkability varies a lot depending on where you buy. Here it mostly does not.

## The park and ride lot, and why it is worth money

This is the amenity that never shows up in a listing description and quietly changes your annual costs.

The Town of Chapel Hill operates a park and ride lot at Southern Village, off US 15-501 South. Chapel Hill Transit runs fare free on every fixed route, funded by the town, Carrboro, and the University rather than by fares. Direct service to campus and downtown.

Why that matters in dollars: UNC prices employee parking permits by salary, from a few hundred dollars a year at the bottom of the scale to well over two thousand at the top. A park and ride permit is a flat couple hundred. For a household commuting to campus or UNC Health, living beside a lot rather than driving in can be worth a four figure difference every year, permanently.

Very few Chapel Hill neighborhoods have a park and ride lot inside them. This one does, and it is the kind of thing buyers only appreciate after they have priced a campus permit.

## Getting to UNC, Duke, and the rest of the Triangle

Two miles to UNC. In practice that is a short drive, a manageable bike ride on the right route, or a bus.

Downtown Chapel Hill and Franklin Street are about five minutes north. Duke and downtown Durham run roughly 20 to 30 minutes depending on traffic. RDU airport is generally 30 to 40 minutes. RTP is reachable but it is a real commute, not a quick one.

Be honest with yourself about which direction your life points. Southern Village is the best of the three planned communities for campus and downtown, and the weakest of the three for I-40, Durham, and the airport. If your household splits between UNC and RTP, [Meadowmont](/neighborhoods/meadowmont-chapel-hill) solves that problem better. Drive your actual route at your actual hour before you decide. The [commute tool on my site](/buyer-tools) gives you a baseline.

## Housing types and price ranges

Southern Village is not one price point. It is several, stacked inside the same neighborhood, which is unusual for Chapel Hill and is a large part of why it holds value.

| Housing type | Approximate count | Current pricing |
|---|---|---|
| Condos | ~230 units | Listings in the $310,000 to $485,000 range |
| Townhomes | ~150 units | Median around $680,000 |
| Single family | ~530 units | Median around $780,500, top of market to $2,550,000 |
| Apartments and rentals | remainder | Varies |

The Zillow Home Value Index for Southern Village sat at **$780,961 as of August 2026, up 0.9 percent year over year**, which reflects the single family side of the neighborhood far more than the condo side. Reported median sale price across all types runs closer to $730,000.

Notice those two numbers disagree by about $50,000. That is normal and it is exactly why you should not shop off a neighborhood average. A condo buyer and a single family buyer in Southern Village are in two different markets that happen to share a name.

Inventory is thin. Fourteen active listings across an entire 1,150 home neighborhood is not much, and it is why homes here often move differently than the broader Chapel Hill market even when the wider market slows down.

*Ask for current comparable sales in your specific housing type before pricing or offering.*

## What town limits cost you, and what they buy

Southern Village sits in Orange County and inside Chapel Hill town limits. Your tax bill carries the Orange County rate plus the Town of Chapel Hill rate, plus the Chapel Hill Carrboro City Schools district tax.

On a $730,000 home inside town limits, that combined rate works out to roughly $8,600 a year. The same house in unincorporated Chatham County, still carrying a Chapel Hill mailing address, would run closer to $5,200.

That is a real gap and you should see it clearly before you decide. What the higher bill buys you: town trash and recycling, town police, town parks and greenways, Chapel Hill Carrboro City Schools, and a two mile trip to campus instead of eight.

Some buyers look at that and take the Chatham County side. If that is you, read the [Briar Chapel guide](/neighborhoods/briar-chapel-chapel-hill), which covers that tradeoff in detail. Others look at it and decide the school district and the location are worth the difference. Both are defensible. What is not defensible is finding out after closing.

## Schools

Southern Village is served by the **Chapel Hill Carrboro City Schools** district.

**Mary Scroggs Elementary sits inside the neighborhood.** Grey Culbreth Middle School is adjacent. High school assignment for this area has historically been Carrboro High School.

**Verify assignment directly with Chapel Hill Carrboro City Schools for the specific property address before you write an offer.** District boundaries and assignments change, they do not follow mailing addresses, and no listing description is a substitute for confirmation from the school system.

## Who Southern Village fits, and who it does not

Most neighborhood guides only tell you the good part. Here is both.

**It works well if you want:**

- To walk to restaurants, coffee, and a movie theater rather than drive
- A short trip to UNC campus, UNC Health, or downtown Chapel Hill
- A park and ride lot inside the neighborhood with fare free service to campus
- Chapel Hill Carrboro City Schools and Chapel Hill town services at the lowest entry price of the three planned communities
- Sidewalks, trails, a pool and clubhouse without maintaining any of it yourself
- Consistent walkability rather than walkability that depends on which street you land on
- Multiple price points in one location, so you can move within the neighborhood later

**It works poorly if you want:**

- A large private yard. Lots here are compact by design and that is not going to change.
- Distance between you and your neighbors. Density is the entire point of the plan.
- The best access to I-40, Durham, RTP, or the airport. That is the east side of town.
- No HOA and no rules. There is an HOA, and the architectural standards that keep the neighborhood looking the way it does are the same standards that will tell you no.
- The most square footage per dollar. You are paying for location and walkability. Chatham County will hand you more house for the same number.
- A quiet street with no through traffic. Homes closest to Market Street get village center activity, and that cuts both ways.
- A leisurely search. Three months of supply means the good ones do not sit.

I would rather you rule this neighborhood out on paper than discover the tradeoff after closing.

## What to check before you make an offer here

**1. Get the full carrying cost, not just the mortgage.** HOA dues vary by housing type, and the recreation club membership is a separate line item, historically around $750 per year for a household membership. Condo and townhome regimes carry their own dues and reserve situations. Add it all up before you decide what you can afford.

**2. Read the HOA documents like they cost money, because they do.** Architectural review, exterior changes, parking rules, and rental restrictions all live in those documents. If you plan to rent the property out at any point, confirm what is permitted before due diligence ends, not after.

**3. Understand alley access and parking.** Rear-loaded garages off alleys are part of the design. Guest parking, leaf and debris handling, and turning radius are all real considerations that a Tuesday afternoon showing will not reveal.

**4. Look at condo association financials separately.** For the condo buildings, reserve funding and any special assessment history matter more to your five year cost than the countertops do.

**5. Confirm the county and the municipality on the parcel record.** Southern Village is in Orange County and inside town limits. Nearby neighborhoods with Chapel Hill mailing addresses are not all the same, and the difference shows up on the tax bill every year.

**6. Move quickly on the right one.** Three months of supply is not a market that rewards a two week think. Have financing sorted before you tour.

## If you are selling in Southern Village

You are in the strongest position of the three planned communities, and that is worth knowing before you set a number.

Three months of supply is a seller's market. Meadowmont and Briar Chapel are both running closer to four, and parts of the wider Chapel Hill area are at eight to fourteen. Thin inventory across 1,150 homes means a correctly priced listing here has genuinely limited competition.

That does not make pricing automatic. Buyers here are almost always comparing you to two things at once: another Southern Village listing, and a larger house in Chatham County for the same money. Your listing has to make the walkability, the school district, and the location tradeoff obvious, or you lose to square footage.

Condition gets scrutinized closely in this neighborhood because buyers paying a location premium expect the house to be ready. Paint, landscaping, and a pre-listing inspection consistently pay for themselves here.

Before you set a number, get a real look at what has actually closed on your street and in your housing type in the last 90 days. The neighborhood's reported median and its Zillow index currently differ by about $50,000, which tells you how much a blended average can mislead. Pricing off one is how sellers leave money on the table.
