*A walkable village built on the old DuBose estate, closer to I-40 than anything else in Chapel Hill, where you trade lot size for a fifteen minute reach to almost everywhere.*

Meadowmont is a 435 acre planned community in Chapel Hill, inside Orange County and inside town limits, built on the former DuBose estate starting in 1999. It holds condos, townhomes, single family homes, and custom homes around a walkable village center, about two miles from UNC and two miles from I-40.

## Every Meadowmont price you have read is wrong

Start here, because it changes how you read everything else on this page and every other page about this neighborhood.

### Three sources. Three medians. Same neighborhood. Same year.

| Source | Reported median | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Redfin, most recent reporting period | $962,500 sale price | Down about 34 percent year over year |
| Homes.com, current | $1,125,000 sale price | Up about 5 percent year over year |
| Homes.com, current list price | $1,192,450 | n/a |

One source says the median is under a million and collapsing by a third. Another says it is over a million and climbing. Neither is lying. Neither is useful.

Earlier in the same year, reported Meadowmont medians ran from roughly $705,000 to over $1.25 million depending on where you looked and when.

### What is actually causing that

Two things, and once you see them you cannot unsee them.

**The sample is tiny.** Redfin's most recent period counted four closed sales. Four. There are currently seven homes on the market in the entire neighborhood. When a neighborhood closes a handful of homes in a reporting window, the median is not a market signal. It is whichever few houses happened to close.

**The housing stock is enormously varied.** A Meadowmont condo trades in the $300Ks. A custom home on the good side of the neighborhood asks $2.4 million. That is a spread of roughly seven times, inside one set of gates, all reported under one neighborhood name.

Put those together and the math does something absurd. Three condos and one custom home close in a quarter and the median lands near $800,000. Three custom homes and one condo close the next quarter and the median lands near $1.4 million. Nothing about the neighborhood changed. The published median moved 75 percent.

This is why I will not quote you a Meadowmont median and tell you it means something. Anyone who does is either not paying attention or hoping you are not.

**What to use instead:** closed sales in your housing type, on your side of the neighborhood, in the last 90 days. Five comparable condos tell you more about a condo than every median ever published about Meadowmont. If you want that pulled for a specific address or price band, [ask me](/contact) and I will run it.

## What Meadowmont actually is

Meadowmont is 435 acres on the east side of Chapel Hill, off NC 54, developed as a mixed-use community rather than a subdivision.

The distinction matters. A subdivision is houses. Meadowmont has houses, condos, townhomes, a grocery store, restaurants, medical offices, a conference center, an elementary school, a ten acre park, and over five miles of trails, all inside the same plan. The design language is New Urbanist, which in practice means sidewalks everywhere, buildings close to the street, parking tucked behind, and a commercial center you can walk to rather than drive to.

It took eight years of planning before ground was broken in 1999. That is unusually long, and it shows. The street grid connects instead of dead-ending, the commercial center has stayed occupied for two decades, and the trail network actually goes places rather than looping decoratively around a retention pond.

What you are buying here is location plus infrastructure. Two miles to UNC in one direction, two miles to I-40 in the other. There is no other Chapel Hill neighborhood that sits that close to both.

## The DuBose estate, and why this land looks different

Meadowmont was built on the DuBose estate, and the history is not decoration. It shaped the plan.

David St. Pierre DuBose and Valinda Hill DuBose built the Meadowmont House in 1933, a Georgian Revival manor with a steel frame and brick veneer. It was one of the first private homes in the United States with central air conditioning. The estate and its outbuildings, including a pool house, garage, and stable, were listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1985.

When the land was developed, the historic core was preserved rather than scraped. UNC's Kenan-Flagler Business School incorporated the property into what is now the Rizzo Conference Center, which still operates on the site.

The practical effect for a buyer: the mature trees, the topography, and the older stone and brick work in parts of the neighborhood are not landscaping installed in 1999. They are the estate. It is the reason Meadowmont reads as older and more settled than a community built in 1999 has any right to look.

## Meadowmont Village and what daily life looks like

Meadowmont Village is the commercial center, and it is the reason a lot of buyers choose here over a comparable house elsewhere in town.

Grocery, restaurants, retail, and offices, arranged on a walkable block rather than a strip center with a parking lot in front. Restaurants have included One Restaurant, Brixx Wood Fired Pizza, Meadowmont Grill, and Sushi Nikko among others, with the usual turnover any commercial center sees over twenty years.

The honest version of walkability here: it depends entirely on where in Meadowmont you buy.

If you are in or near the Village, you can walk to groceries, to dinner, to coffee, to the park, and to the elementary school. That is a genuinely different daily life than driving for all of it.

If you are on the far edge of 435 acres, the Village is a pleasant walk in good weather and a short drive the rest of the time. Still better than most neighborhoods. Not the same thing.

Four hundred and thirty five acres is large. Walk the specific distance from the specific house to the specific things you care about before you decide you are buying walkability.

Four broad categories, and they behave like four separate markets that happen to share an address.

| Single family | Median list around $1,382,500 | The bulk of the neighborhood by land area. |
| Custom homes | Into the $2 millions | Current top of market asks $2,395,000. |

Seven homes are on the market across the entire neighborhood right now, from $795,000 to $2,395,000. Several carry meaningful price reductions, one over $150,000 off the original ask.

Supply sits around four months, which is a balanced market. Worth noting, because nearby Chapel Hill submarkets are running eight to fourteen months of supply, which is firmly a buyer's market. Meadowmont is holding tighter than its surroundings.

What that combination tells you: sellers who priced ambitiously are cutting, and the neighborhood as a whole is still absorbing inventory faster than the areas around it. Both things are true at once. That is normal, and it is invisible if you only look at a median.

## The amenities that do not come with the house

This is the single most common misunderstanding about Meadowmont, and it costs real money.

The UNC Wellness Center at Meadowmont, the YMCA at Meadowmont, and the Meadowmont Swim Club are **separate organizations**. Each has its own membership. None of them is included with homeownership.

Buyers tour the neighborhood, see a fitness center, a YMCA, and a swim club inside the community, and reasonably assume some or all of it comes with the HOA. It does not. Those are memberships you join and pay for, on top of your dues, at whatever the current rates are.

What the community itself provides, and what dues cover, varies by which association your specific home sits in. Meadowmont has a master structure plus separate condo and townhome associations, each carrying its own dues, its own reserves, and its own assessment history.

**Do this every time:** get the full dues figure in writing for the specific address, ask what it actually covers, ask what it does not, and price the memberships you intend to use separately. A household that plans on the Wellness Center and the swim club should build both into the monthly number before deciding what the house costs.

## Getting to UNC, Duke, RTP, and RDU

About two miles to the UNC campus. About two miles to I-40. That second number is what makes this neighborhood different from everywhere else in Chapel Hill.

Duke and downtown Durham typically run 15 to 25 minutes. RDU generally runs 25 to 35. RTP is reachable in a way it is not from the south and west sides of town.

If your household splits, one person at UNC or UNC Health and the other in Durham, RTP, or flying regularly, Meadowmont solves a problem that most Chapel Hill neighborhoods make you choose your way out of. That is a real and specific advantage and it is why the neighborhood holds its pricing.

The tradeoff is NC 54, which carries the traffic you would expect from a road that connects Chapel Hill to I-40. Drive your actual commute at the actual hour before you commit. The [commute tool on my site](/buyer-tools) gives you a baseline. Your own car on a Wednesday morning is the real answer.

## Schools

Meadowmont is served by **Chapel Hill Carrboro City Schools**.

**Rashkis Elementary is located inside the neighborhood** at 601 Meadowmont Lane. Having the elementary school inside the community rather than a drive away is one of the reasons the walkability argument holds here.

Middle and high school assignment for this part of Chapel Hill has shifted over the years and depends on your exact address.

**Verify assignment directly with Chapel Hill Carrboro City Schools for the specific property address before you write an offer.** Boundaries change, assignments do not follow mailing addresses, and no listing description substitutes for confirmation from the school system.

## Meadowmont compared to Southern Village

These two get compared constantly, and for good reason. Both are neo-traditional planned communities in Orange County, inside Chapel Hill town limits. Both have an elementary school inside the neighborhood and a walkable village center. Both trade lot size for walkability.

Here is where they actually differ.

| | Meadowmont | Southern Village |
|---|---|---|
| **Side of town** | East, off NC 54 | South, off 15-501 |
| **Size** | 435 acres | Roughly 300 acres |
| **Density** | More spread out | Denser, concentrated around Market Street |
| **Best access to** | I-40, Durham, RTP, RDU | UNC campus, downtown Chapel Hill, Franklin Street |
| **Village center** | Meadowmont Village: grocery, restaurants, offices | Market Street: restaurants, movie theater, retail |

The short version: if your life points east toward Durham, RTP, or the airport, Meadowmont is the better address. If your life points at campus and downtown, Southern Village is closer to it.

The less obvious difference is density. Southern Village concentrates everything around Market Street, so nearly every home is a short walk to the center. Meadowmont covers 435 acres, so the walkability varies a lot by where you buy. That cuts both ways. More spread means more privacy and more yard on some streets. It also means the Village is not equally close to everyone.

If you are relocating and comparing planned communities, also look at the [Briar Chapel guide](/neighborhoods/briar-chapel-chapel-hill). It is the Chatham County version of the same tradeoff, with a very different tax bill.

## Who Meadowmont fits, and who it does not

Most guides only write the first half. Here is both.

**It works well if you want:**

- The shortest combined reach to UNC, I-40, Durham, RTP, and the airport of any Chapel Hill neighborhood
- A walkable commercial center with a real grocery store, not a strip center you drive to
- An elementary school inside the community
- Five plus miles of trails and a ten acre park without maintaining any of it
- Multiple housing types in one location, so you can move within the neighborhood later
- Chapel Hill Carrboro City Schools and Chapel Hill town services

**It works poorly if you want:**

- A large private lot. Lots here run roughly a tenth to half an acre and that is by design.
- The lowest tax bill available. You are inside Orange County and inside Chapel Hill town limits, which means you pay both rates. On a $1,125,000 home that is roughly $13,300 a year.
- Uniform walkability. On 435 acres, the edge of the neighborhood is not the Village.
- Amenities bundled into your dues. The Wellness Center, YMCA, and swim club are separate memberships.
- The most house per dollar. Chatham County new construction will hand you more square footage for the same money, with a different school district and a much smaller tax bill.
- A published market number you can trust. See the top of this page.

I would rather you rule this neighborhood out on paper than discover the tradeoff after closing.

## What to check before you make an offer here

**1. Get every dues figure in writing for that specific address.** Master association, plus the condo or townhome association if one applies. Then get the reserve study and the last two years of minutes.

**2. Price the memberships separately.** Wellness Center, YMCA, swim club. Decide which ones you will actually use and build them into your monthly number now.

**3. Pull comps in your housing type only.** Not the neighborhood median. Condos compare to condos, townhomes to townhomes. Mixing them produces a number that describes nothing.

**4. Walk the actual distance to the Village.** From that house. Not from the entrance.

**5. Check the build year against the systems.** The neighborhood has been building since 1999. Homes from the earliest phases are at or past the replacement window on roof, HVAC, and water heater.

**6. Confirm the tax picture.** Inside town limits means the Orange County rate plus the Town of Chapel Hill rate, plus the Chapel Hill Carrboro schools district tax. Know the full figure before you set your budget.

**7. Verify school assignment with the district directly.** Every time, for the specific address.

## If you are selling in Meadowmont

Your biggest risk is pricing off a number that describes a different house than yours.

Published Meadowmont medians currently disagree with each other by more than $200,000 and point in opposite directions. If you price a townhome off a median carrying custom home sales, you will overprice. If you price a custom home off a median carrying condo sales, you will leave money on the table. Both happen here regularly.

Price off closed sales in your housing type, on your side of the neighborhood, in the last 90 days. Nothing else.

The current market gives you two things to work with. Supply is running around four months, which is balanced, and it is notably tighter than surrounding Chapel Hill submarkets sitting at eight to fourteen months. That is a real advantage. At the same time, several current listings carry significant price cuts, one over $150,000, which tells you the market is correcting ambitious pricing rather than rewarding it.

Read those together and the strategy is obvious. Price it right the first time and you are in the strongest submarket in the area. Price it hopefully and you will spend two months and a price cut arriving at the number you could have started with.

Condition carries real weight here because buyers paying a Meadowmont premium expect a house that is ready. Paint, landscaping, and a pre-listing inspection consistently pay for themselves.
