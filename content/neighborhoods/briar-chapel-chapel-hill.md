*A Chapel Hill mailing address with a Chatham County tax bill, where you trade the town line for square footage and a school district you did not pick.*

Briar Chapel is a master planned community with a Chapel Hill mailing address located in unincorporated Chatham County, about eight miles south of downtown Chapel Hill. It is served by Chatham County Schools, not Chapel Hill Carrboro, and pays no municipal property tax. The developer has sold the final new home, so resale is now the only way in.

## What Briar Chapel actually is

Briar Chapel is what happens when a developer buys a large piece of land outside a town's limits and builds the thing the town itself will not approve.

Roughly two square miles, about 900 acres of it left as preserved open space, with trails running through most of it. Pools, a clubhouse, parks, sports courts, and a full calendar of community events. Two public schools sitting inside the community. A Central Carolina Community College health sciences campus on site. Builders included David Weekley on the townhomes, Homes by Dickerson, Saussy Burbank, and McNeill Burbank on the custom phases.

It was the first community in the Triangle built to the National Green Building Standard with third party testing, and the developer reports homes running more than 25 percent more efficient than the North Carolina average. Treat that as a developer figure, not an appraisal, but the certification itself is real and it shows up on the utility bill.

What you are actually buying is scale. Amenities that a 40 home subdivision could never carry, on land that Chapel Hill proper ran out of two decades ago.

## Is Briar Chapel in Chapel Hill? The answer costs about $3,000 a year

This is the question that brings most people to a page like this one, and most pages dodge it.

### Where it actually sits

The mailing address says Chapel Hill, NC 27516. The property is in **unincorporated Chatham County**, about eight miles south of downtown Chapel Hill and nine miles north of Pittsboro. It is not inside the Town of Chapel Hill. It is not in Orange County. It is not in the Chapel Hill Carrboro City Schools district.

None of that is a defect. All of it is a set of consequences, and you should know them before you fall in love with a floor plan.

### What the tax difference actually costs

Unincorporated Chatham County means you pay the Chatham County rate plus a fire district rate, and no municipal tax at all. A home inside Chapel Hill town limits pays the Orange County rate plus the Town of Chapel Hill rate.

Run the same $639,680 through both.

| Where the home sits | Combined rate per $100 | Approximate annual tax |
|---|---|---|
| Unincorporated Chatham County, including fire district | About 68.5 to 71.5 cents | $4,380 to $4,575 |
| Inside Town of Chapel Hill, Orange County | 118.05 cents | About $7,551 |

That is a gap of roughly $3,000 a year on an identically priced home. About $250 a month. Over a ten year hold, north of $30,000.

The tradeoff is on the other side of the ledger. No municipal tax means no town trash collection, no town police department, no town parks system, and a longer drive to most of what people move to Chapel Hill for. Some of my clients look at that table and immediately want Chatham County. Some look at it and decide $250 a month is a fair price for being inside town. Both are correct. They are answers to different questions.

What I will not let a client do is make that decision by accident, which is exactly what happens when nobody checks the county before the offer goes in.

*Rates are the approved fiscal year 2026-27 figures for the jurisdictions listed and are subject to change. Fire district rates vary. Verify the applicable rates and the assessed value with the Chatham County tax office for any specific parcel before you write an offer.*

## The community is sold out, and that changes how you buy here

Newland has sold the final new home in Briar Chapel. The community is built out.

Almost every page you will find about Briar Chapel was written before that happened, which is why they all read like a brochure with a quick move-in inventory list at the bottom. That page is describing a purchase you can no longer make.

Here is what being built out actually changes.

**There is no builder to negotiate with.** No incentives, no rate buydowns, no closing cost credits from a sales center trying to hit a quarterly number. Every transaction is now a private seller with an emotional attachment to a number.

**Supply is fixed.** Whatever comes available comes from someone moving out. That is the entire pipeline.

**The villages stop being interchangeable.** When the builder was selling, every phase was new. Now you have homes built across a span of nearly twenty years, in different villages, with different dues, different lot sizes, and different distances to the amenities. A 2009 townhome and a 2023 custom home are in the same community and in two different markets.

**Condition matters more than it used to.** You are no longer comparing a resale to a brand new house down the street. You are comparing it to other resales, and the ones that show well win.

## What the resale market is doing right now

Redfin's July 2026 numbers for Briar Chapel, and what I read in them.

| Metric | July 2026 | Year over year |
|---|---|---|
| Median sale price | $639,680 | Up 1.5% |
| Price per square foot | $272 | Up 3.8% |
| Median days on market | 41 | Up 18 days |
| Homes sold | 40 | Up 24.7% |
| Sale to list ratio | 99.1% | Essentially flat |

Three things worth pulling out of that table, because the headline number is the least interesting part.

**Price per square foot climbed more than twice as fast as the median.** Up 3.8 percent against 1.5 percent. That is not a stalling market. That is a mix shift. More of what sold was smaller, which drags the median down while the actual value of a square foot goes up. If you look only at the median and conclude Briar Chapel flattened out, you have misread it.

**Days on market nearly doubled while sale to list held at 99 percent.** Homes are still closing within a percentage point of asking. They are just taking six weeks instead of three to get there. That is a patience problem, not a pricing problem, and the two require completely different responses from a seller.

**Volume is up almost 25 percent.** That is the built out effect arriving on schedule. Resale is the only supply now, so every buyer who wants in is competing in one pool, and every seller is finally visible to all of them.

*Figures are Redfin neighborhood data as of July 2026 and cover the whole community across every housing type. Your street, your village, and your square footage will not match the average. Get real comparable sales before you price or offer.*

## The HOA stack, and why the number on the listing is not the number

This is the single most common surprise in Briar Chapel, and it is not the HOA's fault. It is a disclosure problem.

Briar Chapel dues come in layers.

**The master assessment** applies to every home in the community and has been reported around $192 per month. That covers the community-wide amenities, the trails, the open space, and the events calendar.

**A village assessment** sits on top of it and varies by which village the home is in, reportedly running somewhere between about $125 and $367 per month. Townhome villages carry more because the association maintains more.

**Encore dues** apply if the home is in the Encore section, an age restricted section at 55 and over with its own amenities, and have been reported to add roughly $230 per month on top.

So a listing that says "HOA $192" can be describing a home with a real carrying cost more than double that. Neither the seller nor the listing agent is necessarily hiding anything. The MLS field just does not have room for a three layer structure.

**Do this every time:** ask for the full monthly stack in writing for the specific address, not for the community. Then ask for the association's reserve study and the last two years of minutes. The Briar Chapel association has put real emphasis on reserve funding and a five to ten year outlook, which is a good sign, and it is also the kind of thing you confirm rather than assume.

*Dues figures are reported third party amounts and change. Confirm the exact current stack for the specific property with the association during your due diligence period.*

## Schools

Briar Chapel is served by **Chatham County Schools**, not Chapel Hill Carrboro City Schools, regardless of the Chapel Hill mailing address.

Two public schools sit inside the community. **Chatham Grove Elementary**, which is LEED Silver certified, and **Margaret B. Pollard Middle**, which was the state's first Gold LEED certified middle school. High school assignment for this area has been **Seaforth High School**, which opened for the 2021-22 year and took students who had previously been zoned to **Northwood High School**.

Charter and independent options in the area include **Woods Charter School** and **Willow Oak Montessori**. There is a Primrose School located at Briar Chapel, and Central Carolina Community College operates a health sciences campus on site.

**Verify assignment directly with Chatham County Schools for the specific property address before you write an offer.** District boundaries change, assignments do not follow mailing addresses, and no listing description substitutes for confirmation from the school system.

## Getting to UNC, Durham, RTP, and RDU

About seven miles to the UNC campus, eight to downtown Chapel Hill, nine south to Pittsboro. On paper that is nothing.

In practice you are on the 15-501 corridor, which is the same road every other Chatham County commuter is on at the same hour. A trip that takes fifteen minutes on a Sunday is a different trip at 8:15 on a Tuesday. Durham and Duke run longer. RTP is a real commute rather than a quick one, and RDU is further still.

This is the tradeoff people underestimate most. The tax savings are permanent and they show up once a year. The commute shows up twice a day.

Before you commit, drive your actual route at the actual hour you would drive it. Not at noon, not on a weekend showing trip. The [commute tool on my site](/buyer-tools) gives you a baseline between two addresses, which is a fine starting point. Your own car on a Wednesday morning is the real answer.

## Who Briar Chapel fits, and who it does not

Most guides only write the first half of this. Here is both.

**It works well if you want:**

- More square footage per dollar than anything comparable inside Chapel Hill town limits
- Trails, pools, a clubhouse, and a full amenity package you do not maintain yourself
- Newer construction with third party green building certification and lower utility costs
- A significantly lower annual property tax bill
- Walkable access to two public schools and a community college campus without leaving the community
- One community with multiple housing types, so you can move within it later without leaving it

**It works poorly if you want:**

- Chapel Hill Carrboro City Schools. You will not get them here at any price.
- A short commute to downtown Chapel Hill, UNC, or anywhere east on I-40. Eight miles on 15-501 is not eight miles at rush hour.
- Town services. No municipal trash, no town police, no town parks. That is what the lower rate buys you out of.
- Minimal rules. There is design review, and exterior changes including landscaping, fencing, solar, and roof work go through it. The standards that keep the community looking the way it does are the same standards that will tell you no.
- A simple, single HOA payment. The stack is real and it is layered.
- New construction. That option closed. Everything here is resale now.

I would rather you rule this community out on paper than discover the tradeoff after closing.

## What to check before you make an offer here

**1. Confirm the county and the fire district on the parcel record.** Not the mailing address, not the listing. The parcel record shows the jurisdictions that actually apply, and that determines your tax bill.

**2. Get the full HOA stack in writing for that specific address.** Master, village, and Encore if applicable. Then get the reserve study and the last two years of minutes.

**3. Read the design guidelines before you plan a single change.** If you have a fence, a solar array, a paint color, or a landscaping plan in mind, find out now whether it is approvable. Not after closing.

**4. Ask which village the home is in and what that village has done on dues.** Village assessments vary widely and they are the part of the stack most likely to move.

**5. Check the build year against the systems.** The community spans nearly two decades. A home from the earliest phases is at or near the replacement window on HVAC, water heater, and roof. Price that in.

**6. Verify school assignment with Chatham County Schools directly.** Every time, for the specific address.

## If you are selling in Briar Chapel

Your competition is no longer the builder. It is the other eleven houses in Briar Chapel that a buyer can tour this weekend.

Two numbers should shape your strategy. Sale to list is holding at 99.1 percent, which means correctly priced homes here still get close to asking. Days on market is up eighteen days year over year, which means they get there slowly. Price like the first number, plan like the second. Sellers who price for a three week sale and then panic in week five give away more than the sellers who priced honestly and waited.

Condition is doing more work than it used to. In a built out community, buyers are comparing your house to the other resales, and the ones that show well take the traffic. Paint, landscaping, and a pre-listing inspection consistently pay for themselves here.

And do not price off the community median. Briar Chapel has townhomes, single family, custom homes on half acre lots, and an age restricted section all rolled into one $639,680 number. That average describes almost nobody. Price off what has actually closed in your village, in your housing type, in the last 90 days.
