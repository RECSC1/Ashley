Emily Wyatt is the founder of Real Estate Concierge Services Company LLC.

She is a solo fractional marketing partner for real estate agents, brokerages, and local businesses. She is not a traditional agency, not a VA, and not a generic website designer.

Her business helps real estate professionals build visibility systems that connect:

- Website structure
- Google Business Profile
- Local SEO
- Lead capture

- Follow-up systems
- AI workflows
- Brand clarity
- Client communication

The goal of every technical asset is simple:

Make the client more visible, more credible, easier to find, easier to contact, and easier to trust.

## Emily’s Working Style

Emily needs practical, plain-English help.

Do not assume she is a developer.

When explaining technical work, use simple language and clear next steps.

Avoid unnecessary developer jargon. If technical terms are required, explain what they mean in normal human language.

## Business Priorities

Every website, landing page, form, tool, or technical fix should support at least one of these goals:

1. Lead generation
2. Local visibility
3. Google search visibility
4. AI search visibility
5. Brand authority
6. Better client follow-up
7. Easier content repurposing

## Critical Payment Rule

No client-specific work begins until payment is received in full.

Before doing any client-specific work, check `CLIENT_STATUS.md`.

If the project is not marked `PAID IN FULL`, do not customize, build, deploy, connect forms, fix client-specific pages, or create handoff materials.

If payment is not complete, only create:

1. A scope summary
2. A payment-required kickoff checklist
3. A client-facing next steps email
4. A list of what work begins after payment clears

Internal RECSC templates and reusable assets can be worked on without client payment status.

## Website Build Standards

For every real estate website, check:

1. Do all buttons go somewhere useful?
2. Do all forms work?
3. Are forms clearly named?
4. Are form submissions routed correctly?
5. Is there a success message after submission?
6. Are fake or placeholder tools clearly labeled?
7. Are page titles and meta descriptions present?
8. Is the site mobile-friendly?
9. Are service areas clear?
10. Is the site safe to show to a paying client?

## Real Estate Website Feature Rules

Do not imply that a feature is fully live unless it actually works.

Be careful with anything labeled:

- AI Home Search
- Map Search
- School Rating Search
- Home Valuation
- Property Alerts
- Buyer Dashboard
- Seller Dashboard
- IDX Search
- MLS Search
- Mortgage Calculator
- Community Landmarks
- Virtual Staging
- Saved Search
- Account Login
- Automated Alerts

If the feature is not truly connected to a backend, MLS, IDX, live data source, or automation system, label it professionally as informational, request-based, or concierge-style.

Example wording:

“This tool is designed to start the conversation. Once submitted, Ashley will personally follow up with next steps.”

or

“Property information and recommendations are provided through direct follow-up, not an automated live search portal.”

## Netlify Expectations

When working with Netlify-hosted sites, always document:

- Base directory
- Build command
- Publish directory
- Whether `netlify.toml` is needed
- Whether Netlify Forms are configured
- Which forms need testing
- Whether notifications are set up
- What Emily needs to test before sending the site to the client

For static HTML sites, the publish directory may often be the root folder or the folder containing `index.html`.

For Vite/React builds, the publish directory is often `dist`, but only if the build command actually creates `dist`.

Do not guess. Inspect the project files.

## Forms

For real estate client websites, forms should be clear and specific.

Useful form names include:

- contact
- buyer-consultation-request
- seller-consultation-request
- relocation-inquiry
- home-valuation-request
- neighborhood-guide-request
- property-question
- showing-request

When possible, add hidden fields to help identify where the lead came from.

Examples:

- page_name
- lead_source
- form_type
- client_name

Every form should have a clear success message.
